What’s inside

The template has four parts, in the order you fill them in:

  • Fixed this week — meetings, classes, appointments, travel. Anything with a set time.
  • Three outcomes — what has to be true by Friday evening. Outcomes, not activities: “Homepage copy approved”, not “work on website”.
  • Task list — every task with an estimate in minutes, a project and, if it has one, a deadline.
  • Day columns — Monday to Sunday, where tasks land next to the fixed events, with a running total of planned hours per day.

The CSV version has one row per task or event (title, date, start, end, duration, project, priority, notes), so you can import it into a calendar or task app.

When to use it (and when not)

Use a weekly planner when your work spans several projects and the week has a shape: a few meetings, a couple of deadlines, some deep work. It helps most on Sunday evening or Monday morning, before the inbox decides for you.

Skip it if your days are fully scheduled by someone else (shift work, a school timetable) — a daily planner is enough. And if you need to see months, not days, use a project timeline instead.

How to use it

  1. Copy in the fixed events. Open your calendar and write every timed commitment into its day column.
  2. Write three outcomes. If a task does not serve one of them, it is optional this week.
  3. Dump all tasks. Add an estimate to each. Round up: 20 minutes becomes 30.
  4. Check capacity per day. Subtract meetings from your working hours. Plan no more than about 70% of what is left; the rest absorbs overruns and surprises.
  5. Place tasks on days. Deadline tasks first, then outcome tasks on your best-energy days, then the rest.
  6. Re-check on Wednesday. Move what slipped, cut what no longer matters.

On paper this takes about 20 minutes. Our guide to planning your week walks through it in more detail.

Questions, answered.

Should I plan the week on Sunday or Monday?

Whichever you will actually do. Sunday evening gives you a calm start; Monday at 9:00 gives you the latest information. Keep it to 20 minutes either way.

How many tasks should a weekly plan hold?

Fewer than you think. Add up the estimates and compare them with your free hours after meetings. If the total is above roughly 70% of that time, cut or defer until it fits.

What if I don’t know how long a task takes?

Guess, then round up. After two or three weeks, compare your estimates with what happened and adjust. The planning fallacy is real: most people underestimate.

Can I use the CSV with Google Calendar?

The CSV uses plain columns (title, date, start, end, duration, project, priority, notes). Rename the headers to what your calendar or task app expects, then import.

Does Needt plan the weekend too?

Only if you let it. You set your working hours, and auto-scheduling only places tasks inside them.
