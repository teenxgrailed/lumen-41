What’s inside

  • Positioning line — “For [who], [brand] is the [category] that [difference].”
  • Three adjectives with their opposites, to stop drift: “calm, not sleepy”.
  • Palette with roles — base, text, primary, accent, with rough proportions (60 / 25 / 10 / 5).
  • Type pairing — a heading face and a body face, with one sample line.
  • Photo direction — light, framing, subjects, what never appears.
  • Do / don’t pairs — four side-by-side examples.
  • Touchpoints — where the brand shows up first: site, packaging, social avatar.

When to use it (and when not)

Use it when starting a brand, naming a product line, or refreshing a look that grew by accident. It is also a solid brief for a designer: you hand over decisions, not a vague feeling.

It is not a final identity system. Logos, spacing rules and accessibility checks on color contrast come later. For a one-off project look, a regular moodboard is quicker.

How to use it

  1. Write the positioning line first. If you cannot, the board will not fix that.
  2. Pick three adjectives and write their “not” next to each.
  3. Collect references by category: 4–6 for color, 3–4 for type, 6–8 for photography.
  4. Choose the palette and assign a role and proportion to each color.
  5. Mock two touchpoints quickly — a social avatar and a product label — to test the choices.
  6. Write the do / don’t pairs from what failed in the mocks.
  7. Review after a week. Brand choices made in one sitting often look different on day seven.

Questions, answered.

How is a brand moodboard different from a regular moodboard?

A regular moodboard sets a look for one project. A brand moodboard sets rules that repeat: color roles, type, photo direction, and what never appears.

How many brand colors do I need?

Usually four: a base, a text color, a primary and an accent. More colors get harder to use consistently.

Do I need a designer to use this?

No, but it makes working with one faster and cheaper. You arrive with decisions and references instead of a feeling.

Should I check color contrast?

Yes, before the colors go on a website. Text and background pairs need enough contrast to be readable; check them with a contrast checker.

Can I link my Pinterest board?

In Needt, yes: link a Pinterest board to a Board and keep your saved pins next to the palette and notes.
