What’s inside

  • Top three — the tasks that make today a good day if nothing else gets done.
  • Hour column — 08:00 to 18:00 in 30-minute rows. Meetings go in first, then your top three, then smaller tasks.
  • Quick list — five-minute items (replies, a call, a form) to batch into one slot.
  • Shutdown — what got done, what moves to tomorrow, and the first task for the morning.

When to use it (and when not)

A daily planner fits days with a mix of meetings and open time — most knowledge work, studying, running a small shop. It is the right tool when the week is roughly clear but each day gets eaten by small things.

It is the wrong tool for planning across days. If Monday’s plan keeps spilling into Thursday, start with a weekly planner and use this page only to place today’s share.

How to use it

  1. Write in today’s meetings with their exact times.
  2. Pick your top three. At least one should move a project forward, not just clear a deadline.
  3. Place the top three in your longest free stretches, hardest task first if mornings are your best hours.
  4. Batch the quick list into one 30-minute slot, usually after lunch.
  5. Leave one empty hour. Something will run over.
  6. At the end of the day, fill the shutdown box and write tomorrow’s first task.

If you want the five-minute version, read plan your day in five minutes.

Questions, answered.

Why only three top tasks?

Three is small enough to finish on a day with meetings and big enough to matter. If you finish early, pull from the quick list or tomorrow.

Should I plan in 15- or 30-minute blocks?

Thirty minutes is easier to keep on paper. Use 15-minute rows only if your day is full of short calls.

What goes in the shutdown note?

Done, moved, and the first task for tomorrow. Writing the first task down makes the morning start faster.

Can I print it?

Yes. The Markdown file prints cleanly from most notes apps; one day fits on a page.

What if my day blows up by 10:00?

Keep the top three and redo the hour column from now. Whatever no longer fits moves to tomorrow; write it in the shutdown box so it does not get lost.

How is this different from the time-blocking template?

The daily planner is a light plan with a top three. The time-blocking schedule gives every hour a job and defends focus blocks across the week.
