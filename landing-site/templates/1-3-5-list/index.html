1-3-5 list template

A 1-3-5 list caps your day at nine things: one big task, three medium tasks and five small ones. It forces a realistic mix, so the day has one real accomplishment and the small stuff still gets done. This template adds time ranges for each size, so you can check the nine fit around your meetings.

What’s inside

  • One big — 90 minutes or more. The task that makes the day count.
  • Three medium — 30 to 60 minutes each.
  • Five small — 5 to 15 minutes each: replies, bookings, quick fixes.
  • Fit check — the total against your free hours.
  • Swap rule — what to do when something urgent arrives.

Where it comes from: the 1-3-5 rule was popularized by the career site The Muse in the early 2010s as a way to make daily to-do lists realistic. It has no formal research behind it; it is a practical rule of thumb.

When to use it (and when not)

Use it if your lists are either 25 items long or a single giant task. The fixed sizes push you to break big work down and to stop treating every small thing as a priority.

It fits less well on days with more than four hours of meetings — try 1-2-3 instead. And it does not rank tasks; if order matters more than mix, use the Ivy Lee method.

How to use it

  1. Check tomorrow’s free time. A full 1-3-5 day needs about 4 to 5 hours outside meetings.
  2. Pick the one big task. If it is bigger than three hours, take one chunk of it.
  3. Pick three medium tasks. At least one should be something you have been putting off.
  4. Pick five small tasks. Batch them into one or two slots rather than scattering them.
  5. Do the big one first if mornings are your best hours.
  6. Swap, do not add. When something urgent comes in, it replaces a task of the same size.

How Needt fills it in

Needt reads sizes from your estimates. Type the nine tasks with durations — “wireframe 2h”, “edit newsletter 45 min”, “book courier 5 min” — and auto-scheduling places the big one in your highest-energy slot, the medium ones in the next longest gaps, and the small ones together where short gaps are left. If a meeting moves, it rebuilds the day and shows what moved.

To build the list itself, ask:

You can save a Workspace view filtered to today’s tasks, sorted by estimate, to see your 1-3-5 at a glance.

Questions, answered.

Who came up with the 1-3-5 list?

It was popularized by The Muse, a career site, in the early 2010s. It is a rule of thumb, not a researched method.

What counts as big, medium and small?

A practical split is 90 minutes or more for big, 30 to 60 for medium and under 15 for small. Adjust to your day.

What if I finish early?

Pull the next task from tomorrow’s list, or stop. A finished list is the goal, not a full day.

Can I use 1-3-5 for a week?

Yes, as a weekly mix: one big outcome, three medium projects, five small chores. Then plan days from it.

What if the big task does not fit in one day?

Take one piece that does: “outline the report”, not “write the report”.
