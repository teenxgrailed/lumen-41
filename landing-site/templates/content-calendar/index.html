What’s inside

  • Pipeline table — title, channel (blog, newsletter, YouTube, podcast), format, status, publish date, draft-by date.
  • Production steps — outline, draft, edit, assets, publish, each with an estimate.
  • Themes per month — one line so pieces connect instead of scattering.
  • Idea backlog — a parking lot that stays off the calendar until it has a date.

It covers long-form content. For daily posts across platforms, use the social media content calendar.

When to use it (and when not)

Use it once you publish on a schedule — a weekly newsletter, two blog posts a month, a video every other Friday — and pieces keep getting written the night before.

If you publish when inspiration strikes and that works, keep the idea backlog and skip the dates. A calendar adds pressure that only helps if you want consistency.

How to use it

  1. Set your cadence. Write publish dates for the next 6–8 weeks before choosing topics.
  2. Assign topics from the backlog to dates, following the month’s theme.
  3. Work backwards. Draft-by is publish date minus your edit time; a newsletter might need 3 days, a video 7.
  4. Estimate production steps. A 1,500-word post might be outline 30m, draft 120m, edit 60m, images 30m.
  5. Put those steps into your week as real time, not just reminders.
  6. Update status on Fridays: Idea, Outlined, Drafted, Edited, Scheduled, Published.

The content calendar workflow guide goes deeper on batching.

How Needt fills it in

Keep the pipeline table as a database in a Needt doc and link it to a Content project. Each production step becomes a task with an estimate and a deadline at the draft-by date. Auto-scheduling spreads the steps across free time before the deadline, so “draft October newsletter, 2h, by Oct 20” lands on a morning with space instead of the night before.

If a shoot moves or a client project eats Wednesday, the steps shift and Needt shows what moved. To build the steps from a row, ask:

Turn the “Packaging behind the scenes” video into outline, script, film and edit tasks, finished by Oct 28.

Pair it with a moodboard per series for visual direction.

Questions, answered.

How far ahead should a content calendar go?

Six to eight weeks of dates is enough for most solo creators and small teams. Further out, topics change too often to be worth planning.

What is a draft-by date?

The date a piece must be fully drafted so editing and assets fit before publishing. It is the date that actually drives your work.

Should I include ideas without dates?

Keep them in a separate backlog. Undated ideas on the calendar make it look full when nothing is scheduled.

Can I share it with a collaborator?

The Markdown and CSV files work in any shared folder or sheet. In Needt, keep the pipeline in a doc linked to the project.

How many hours does a blog post take?

It varies a lot by person and topic. Log your production steps for three or four pieces and use your own averages; those numbers are what make the calendar realistic.

Does this work for podcasts?

Yes. Swap the production steps for booking guest, record, edit and show notes, each with its own estimate.
