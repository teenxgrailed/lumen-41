Glossary / Deep work

What is deep work?

Deep work is uninterrupted focus on hard, valuable work: writing, designing, coding, solving. Cal Newport coined the term in 2016 and set it against shallow work, the logistics and messages that fill most days.

The definition, expanded

Newport defines deep work as professional activity done in a state of distraction-free concentration that pushes your cognitive capabilities to their limit. Shallow work is the opposite: tasks that don’t need much focus and are easy to replicate, often done while distracted. Both are necessary. The argument is that deep work is getting rarer while becoming more valuable, so it needs protected time.

Where it comes from

Cal Newport, a computer science professor at Georgetown University, introduced the term in his blog and then in the 2016 book Deep Work: Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World. The book describes several ways to schedule it, from whole days or weeks away (“monastic”) to a fixed daily slot (“rhythmic”).

How it works

  1. Pick a schedule you can keep: most people start rhythmic, a fixed block at the same time each day.
  2. Define the output before you start: “1,000 words of chapter 3”.
  3. Remove triggers: notifications off, phone in another room, one window open.
  4. Start with 60 to 90 minutes. Extend as it gets easier.
  5. Batch shallow work into separate slots so it doesn’t leak in.

An example

Alex’s week, rhythmic style:

  • Mon–Thu 08:30–10:30 · Deep work: Website rebuild #Website
  • Daily 12:30–13:00 · Email and messages
  • Afternoons · Client calls #Clients
  • Friday · No deep block; reviews and admin

Monday’s block has one goal: “new navigation working on mobile”. Slack is closed until 10:30.

When it doesn’t work

Many jobs don’t allow two dark hours: support, management, parenting at home. Critics point out that the book is written from a position with unusual control over one’s schedule. Deep work also needs a well-defined task. Two hours on “think about strategy” often turns into two hours of browsing. Shallow work doesn’t disappear either; it just moves, and needs its own home.

Questions, answered.

Deep work vs flow?

Flow is a mental state you can’t schedule directly. Deep work is a practice you can: a protected block with one hard task. Deep work often leads to flow, but doesn’t require it.

How many hours of deep work a day?

Newport, drawing on research into deliberate practice, puts the daily limit at around four hours even for experienced people. Starting with one or two protected hours is more realistic.

Is email ever deep work?

Rarely. Writing a hard, high-stakes message can be, but replying through an inbox is the textbook example of shallow work.

Does deep work need a long block?

Usually 60 minutes or more, since getting into a hard problem takes time. Short slots are better spent on shallow tasks.
