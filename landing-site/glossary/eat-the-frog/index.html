Glossary / Eat the frog

What does eat the frog mean?

To eat the frog is to do your hardest, most important task first, before email or easy wins. The frog is the task you are most likely to put off and the one that matters most if done.

Eat the frog means doing your most important and most avoided task first, before anything else, so the rest of the day is easier and the hard task can’t be postponed again.Origin: Popularized by Brian Tracy’s 2001 book Eat That Frog!, built on a quote often attributed to Mark Twain; Quote Investigator found no evidence Twain said it.

The definition, expanded

Two conditions make a task a frog: it matters, and you are avoiding it. A dull task that doesn’t matter isn’t a frog; it’s a candidate for deletion. A big task you are excited about isn’t a frog either. The frog is usually a phone call, a hard email, a first draft, or a decision with no clean answer.

The rule is about order. Eat it before the day fills up with reasons not to.

Where it comes from

The phrase took off with Brian Tracy’s 2001 book Eat That Frog!, which opens with a line attributed to Mark Twain about eating a live frog first thing in the morning. The attribution is disputed. Quote Investigator found no evidence Twain said it and traces the idea to older sayings, including one attributed to the 18th-century French writer Nicolas Chamfort about swallowing a toad each morning.

How it works

  1. The evening before, pick one frog. Only one.
  2. Make it small enough to finish in one sitting, or name the first concrete piece.
  3. Prepare what you need: open the doc, find the phone number.
  4. Start on it before opening email or messages.
  5. When it’s done, the rest of the list is the reward.

An example

Alex has been avoiding a call to a client about a late payment for a week. Wednesday’s plan:

  • 08:45–09:00 · Call Dana about invoice #112 #Clients (the frog)
  • 09:00–10:30 · Website: build pricing page #Website
  • 10:30–11:00 · Email batch

The call takes eight minutes. The pricing page gets Alex’s full attention instead of sharing it with a dreaded call.

When it doesn’t work

Not everyone does their best thinking at 08:00. If your energy peaks after lunch, a hard creative frog first thing may get done badly. Some frogs depend on other people who aren’t available early. And picking a frog every single day can turn into a habit of finding problems. Use it for avoided work, not as a rule for every morning.

Eating the frog in Needt

Mark the frog with priority Urgent or High and set its energy to High: call Dana about invoice, 15 min, today #Clients. Auto-scheduling uses energy when placing tasks, and it respects deadlines and priority, so the frog lands early in your free time. It appears at the top of Home as the first thing of the day. If your best hours are later, schedule it there; the rule is “first among your focus hours”, not “before breakfast”.

Agent prompt: “What’s the task I’ve postponed the most this month? Put it first thing tomorrow.”

Questions, answered.

Did Mark Twain say “eat a live frog”?

There is no evidence he did. Quote Investigator traced the idea to older sayings, including one attributed to Nicolas Chamfort about swallowing a toad.

What if I have two frogs?

Tracy’s advice is to eat the ugliest one first. In practice: pick the one with a deadline or the one blocking other work.

Does the frog have to be the biggest task?

No. It is the most important task you are avoiding. A 10-minute call you have dodged for a week is a better frog than a big task you already enjoy.

Eat the frog vs Ivy Lee method?

Ivy Lee ranks six tasks for tomorrow and you work them in order. Eat the frog only cares about the first one. They combine well: make the frog task number one.
