Glossary

Planning, in plain words.

Short, sourced explanations of the methods people use to plan their time — and how each one works in Needt.

Term

Context switching

Context switching is shifting attention from one task to another, and the time and focus lost each time your mind has to drop one set of goals and load a different one.

Read moreTerm

Deep work

Deep work is focused, distraction-free effort on a cognitively demanding task, the kind that pushes your skills and produces work that is hard to replicate, as defined by Cal Newport.

Read moreTerm

Eat the frog

Eat the frog means doing your most important and most avoided task first, before anything else, so the rest of the day is easier and the hard task can’t be postponed again.

Read moreTerm

Eisenhower Matrix

The Eisenhower Matrix is a two-by-two grid that sorts tasks by whether they are urgent and whether they are important, giving four actions: do now, schedule, delegate, or drop.

Read moreTerm

Getting Things Done (GTD)

Getting Things Done (GTD) is David Allen’s method of capturing every commitment outside your head, deciding each one’s next physical action, and organizing those actions into trusted lists you review weekly.

Read moreTerm

Parkinson's law

Parkinson’s law is the observation that work expands to fill the time available for its completion: give a task a week and it takes a week, give it a day and it often fits.

Read moreTerm

Planning fallacy

The planning fallacy is the tendency to underestimate how long our own tasks will take, even when we know similar tasks ran over before, because we picture the plan instead of past outcomes.

Read moreTerm

Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is a time management method that splits work into 25-minute focused intervals called pomodoros, each followed by a short break, with a longer break after every four.

Read moreTerm

Task batching

Task batching is grouping similar small tasks, such as email, calls, invoices or errands, and doing them together in one dedicated block instead of scattering them across the day.

Read moreTerm

Timeboxing

Timeboxing is deciding in advance how much time a task gets, working on it only inside that window, and stopping when the time runs out, finished or not.
