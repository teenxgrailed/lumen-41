The problem: five jobs, one calendar

Monday you’re the engineer. Then a support ticket makes you the help desk, a lead replies and you’re sales, the accountant needs receipts and you’re finance. By Friday the feature you planned to ship is 30% done and you can’t say where the week went.

Nobody tells a solo founder no. Every role is urgent to someone. The only way to protect product time is to give it hours on the calendar and see what breaks when you don’t.

A week planned in Needt

Alex is building a small SaaS alone. Projects: Product, Sales, Ops.

  • Mon–Thu 08:30–11:30 Product blocks: onboarding redesign, then the billing bug. High energy, first thing.
  • Mon 14:00–15:00 Two customer interviews via the booking page.
  • Tue 13:00–13:45 Support inbox: three emails turned into tasks, two answered on the spot.
  • Wed 15:00 Investor intro call (added Tuesday night).
  • Thu 14:00–15:00 Outreach: 10 personal emails to trial users.
  • Fri 10:00–11:00 Ops: receipts, VAT export, renew domain.
  • Fri 16:00–16:30 Weekly review and changelog draft.

When the investor call lands on Wednesday afternoon, Needt moves the outreach block to Thursday and the changelog draft to Friday, and keeps the morning product blocks untouched. Alex sees the trade before accepting it.

Founder weeks, three ways

Launch week

Product work shrinks to bug fixes; outreach, support and the launch post grow. Add the launch as a deadline and work backward. Needt shows whether the launch-day checklist fits or needs to start a day earlier.

Fundraising weeks

Investor calls are spread across your days. Use a booking page with a few fixed windows so they cluster, and keep at least two morning product blocks per week. If those blocks disappear, you’ll see it.

Quiet build weeks

No calls. Treat it as a gift: give product four-hour blocks and batch support into one daily slot after lunch. The deep work idea fits here better than anywhere.

The Friday number

On Friday, compare planned product hours with what you actually did. One number, every week. It tells you more about the company than most dashboards.

What Needt doesn’t do

Needt isn’t a CRM, an issue tracker or a team workspace. It won’t track deal stages, manage a backlog for engineers or handle customer support tickets. Keep Linear or GitHub Issues for the backlog, a CRM such as HubSpot for pipeline, and your help desk for support. Needt decides when you work on each. When you hire, your team will still need a shared tool; Needt stays your personal plan.

Questions, answered.

Can Needt replace my CRM or issue tracker?

No. Needt plans your time; it doesn’t manage deal pipelines or engineering backlogs. Keep those tools and schedule the work from them in Needt.

Can I plan from Claude or ChatGPT?

Yes. Connect the Needt MCP server at https://mcp.needt.app/u/your-name and ask your assistant to add tasks, list what’s overdue or re-plan the day.

How do I stop sales and support eating product time?

Give product work long estimates and High energy so it lands in your best hours, and batch email into fixed blocks. The week view shows when one role is crowding out another.

Can investors or customers book time with me?

Yes, through booking pages. Free includes one, Pro unlimited.

Does Needt work for a team?

Needt is a personal planner. Each person can have an account, but there’s no shared team workspace or task assignment.

Is my data used to train AI models?

The hosted agent uses providers that don’t retain or train on your data. You can also bring your own AI key on any plan. See the security page for details.

Can I export everything if I switch?

Yes. You can export your data as JSON at any time.
