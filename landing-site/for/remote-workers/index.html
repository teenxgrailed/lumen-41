The problem: the day has no edges

There’s no commute to start the day or end it. You open the laptop at 08:00 to "just check messages" and close it at 19:30. In between: five video calls, threads in three channels, a doc to review and the real work squeezed into whatever’s left. Lunch happens at the desk.

Async work sounds flexible, but without a plan it fills every hour. Remote work doesn’t need more discipline. It needs a plan with a start, breaks and an end.

A day planned in Needt

Alex works remotely on a distributed team. Calls come from Outlook.

  • 08:45–09:00 Plan the day in Home. Messages after, not before.
  • 09:00–11:00 Focus: quarterly report draft.
  • 11:00–11:15 Break: walk around the block.
  • 11:15–12:00 Async block: reply to threads, review two docs.
  • 12:00–13:00 Lunch away from the desk.
  • 13:00–15:00 Three video calls (from the calendar).
  • 15:15–16:15 Report: charts and edits.
  • 16:15–17:00 Wrap-up: write tomorrow’s top three, close the laptop.

The 14:00 call runs to 15:30. Needt moves the report edits to 15:40, trims nothing from the wrap-up and pushes the second doc review to tomorrow. The day still ends at 17:00.

Remote setups, different shapes

Heavy-meeting days

Some days are mostly calls. Don’t fight it: plan one short focus block and small tasks around the calls. Put the big work on the days with fewer meetings, and check the week view on Monday to find them.

Async-first teams

Fewer meetings, more messages. Batch replies into two slots, late morning and late afternoon. The rest of the day is focus time, and colleagues still get answers the same day. See task batching.

Working from a café or a second city

Install Needt on your laptop and phone. The plan is the same everywhere, so you don’t rebuild it when you switch locations.

Parents working from home

School pickup and lunch at home are events in your personal calendar. With both calendars connected on Pro, Needt plans work around them and never books a deep-work block across pickup time.

What Needt doesn’t do

Needt doesn’t replace your team tools. There’s no team chat, no status sync with Slack or Teams, no shared project board and no video calls. It won’t set an out-of-office or block the calendar for colleagues on its own. Keep Slack or Teams for communication and your team’s project tool for shared work; use Needt for how your own day is shaped. If your company needs team-wide focus-time policies, Reclaim leans further toward that; see Needt vs Reclaim.

Questions, answered.

Does Needt work with Microsoft 365 and Teams calendars?

Needt connects to Outlook/Microsoft 365 calendars, so Teams meetings on that calendar show up as busy time. There’s no Teams chat integration.

Can I see my work and personal calendars together?

Yes, on Pro, which connects unlimited calendars. The Free plan includes one.

Can colleagues see my Needt plan?

No. Your tasks are private. Colleagues see your calendar as they normally do; you can share a booking page if you want them to pick a time.

How does Needt protect focus time?

Give deep work a long estimate and High energy. Needt places it in the longest free gaps between meetings and re-plans when a meeting moves.

Does it update my Slack status?

No. Needt doesn’t integrate with Slack. Use focus sessions in Needt and set your status in Slack yourself.

Can I plan breaks?

Yes. Add breaks as short tasks, like a 15-minute walk, and they get slots in the day like any other task.
