The problem: every client thinks they’re the only one

Client A wants revisions by Wednesday. Client B books a "quick call" that takes the whole morning. Client C sends feedback by email at 22:00. Meanwhile you still owe two invoices, a tax estimate and a proposal for a lead that might become Client D.

Your to-do list has all of it. What it doesn’t have is time. Revisions take three hours whether or not you planned them, and the work you don’t schedule ends up in the evening. The admin that pays you, invoicing and follow-ups, slides to the end of the month.

A week planned in Needt

Alex runs a small design-and-copy practice with three active clients, kept as projects under #Clients.

  • Monday 09:00–12:00 Northwind homepage revisions. Deadline Wednesday, so Needt put it first.
  • Monday 14:00–14:30 Discovery call with a new lead, booked through Alex’s booking page.
  • Tuesday 09:00–11:00 Harbor brand copy, part 1 of 2.
  • Tuesday 16:00–16:45 Invoices for October. A fixed admin block Alex adds every Monday morning.
  • Wednesday A client email arrives: "Can you also resize the banners?" Alex turns it into a task, 45 minutes, due Friday. Needt finds a slot Thursday at 15:00.
  • Friday 15:00–15:45 Weekly review: what’s billable, what’s late, what’s next.

When Client B moves their call from Tuesday to Wednesday at 10:00, Needt shifts the Harbor copy part 2 to Wednesday afternoon and shows the change. Alex confirms in one tap.

Three weeks every freelancer knows

The scope creep week

A client asks for "a few extra pages". Before replying, add them as tasks with honest estimates. If the week view shows the work landing on Saturday, you have your answer, and a reason to quote a new deadline or a new fee.

The feast week

Two projects start the same Monday. Put both deadlines in and let Needt lay them out. Where they collide, you’ll see it on day one instead of day four, and you can tell one client "Thursday" instead of "Tuesday" while there’s still time.

The famine week

No client work booked. Use the space for the business: portfolio, outreach, a proposal template. Give those tasks deadlines too, or they’ll stay "someday" until the next busy week.

Month-end

Add "send October invoices" with a deadline of the 1st, and a reminder for payment follow-ups two weeks later. Needt doesn’t do the invoicing, but it makes sure the hour for it exists.

What Needt doesn’t do

Needt doesn’t send invoices, track billable hours or hold contracts. Keep your invoicing tool (Bonsai, FreshBooks, your accountant’s system) and use Needt to make sure the invoicing actually happens. There’s no client portal either: clients don’t log in to Needt to see progress. If you need billable time reports, pair Needt with a time tracker such as Toggl.

Questions, answered.

Can Needt track my billable hours?

No. Needt plans time, it doesn’t record billable hours or create invoices. Pair it with a time tracker or invoicing tool and schedule the invoicing itself in Needt.

Can clients book calls with me?

Yes. Booking pages let people pick a time that’s free on your calendar. Free includes one booking page, Pro unlimited.

How do I keep clients separate?

Make a project per client and add tasks to it, like #Northwind. Each project keeps its own task list, and the calendar shows every client’s work together.

Can I turn a client email into a task?

Yes. Connect Gmail, Outlook or IMAP, open the email and Needt suggests a task with title, estimate and deadline. You edit and save it.

What happens when a client moves a call?

When the calendar event moves, Needt rebuilds the rest of your schedule around it and shows what moved. You confirm the change.

Can clients see my plan?

No. Needt is a personal planner with no client portal. You can share a moodboard link, but tasks and calendar stay private.

Is there a one-time price?

Yes, Lifetime is $149 once, limited to 300 seats. Pro is $7/month or $59/year.
