Plan a content calendar you’ll ship

To plan a content calendar, choose a cadence you can sustain, define 3–4 content pillars, fill a month of publish dates, then work backwards and schedule the production work (scripting, shooting, editing) into your real calendar. Most content calendars fail because they plan publish dates, not the hours behind them.

You need two calendars, not one

A publish calendar says what goes out when: “Thu Oct 15, Instagram reel, packing an order”. A production calendar says when the work happens: “Mon 14:00–15:00, shoot 3 reels”. Most templates only cover the first. That’s why posts slip: the slot to publish exists, the hour to make it doesn’t.

The workflow below builds both. Start from the content calendar template or the social media content calendar if you want a ready grid.

The workflow in six steps

  1. Pick a cadence you can keep for 12 weeks. Two posts a week you actually ship beats daily posting for nine days. Write it down: “Instagram Tue + Thu, newsletter every other Sunday”.
  2. Define 3–4 pillars. Recurring themes your audience expects. For a small shop: Behind the scenes, Product, Tips, Customer stories. Rotate them so no week is all promotion.
  3. Fill four weeks of publish dates. One row per post: date, channel, pillar, working title. Leave one slot a month empty for something timely.
  4. Estimate production per format. Be honest. Example: reel = 30 min script + 45 min shoot + 60 min edit. Newsletter = 90 min write + 20 min layout. Carousel = 60 min.
  5. Batch production into blocks. Shoot three reels in one 2-hour session instead of three 45-minute ones. Batching cuts setup time; see task batching.
  6. Schedule the work backwards from each publish date. Edits done at least 2 days before posting; shoots a week ahead. Put these blocks on your real calendar next to meetings.

Worked example: Alex’s Shop content

Alex runs a small online shop beside client work. Cadence: Instagram Tue + Thu, newsletter every other Sunday. Pillars: Making-of, New product, Styling tip.

Tue Oct 13Reel: packing an order (Making-of)Shot Wed Oct 7 batch; edit Fri Oct 9, 10:00–11:00
Thu Oct 15Carousel: 3 ways to style the tote (Styling tip)Tue Oct 13, 14:00–15:00

Tue Oct 20Reel: new mug glaze test (New product)Batch shoot Wed Oct 14, 15:00–17:00 (3 reels); edit Fri Oct 16, 14:00–15:00

On Tuesday Oct 13 a client call runs over into the 14:00 carousel block. Because the carousel publishes Thursday, there’s room: it moves to Wednesday 11:00. If production had been planned the night before posting, the post would simply not exist.

Production time, by format

Starting estimates for a solo creator. Time your own first five pieces of each and replace these numbers with yours.

FormatStepsStarting estimate
Short video / reelScript, shoot, edit, caption2–3 h each, ~1.5 h when batched by three
CarouselOutline, design, caption60–90 min
NewsletterOutline, write, layout, test send2–2.5 h
Blog postOutline, draft, edit, images3–5 h
Story / quick postShoot + post10–20 min

Multiply by your weekly cadence and compare to your free hours. If two reels and a newsletter take 6 hours and you have 4, change the cadence now, not in week three.

Make one piece do three jobs

The cheapest post is one you already made. Plan one “anchor” piece per week or fortnight, then cut it down:

  1. Anchor: the newsletter or a longer video (2–3 hours).
  2. Cut 1: a carousel of the three main points (30 minutes, since the thinking is done).
  3. Cut 2: a 20-second reel from the B-roll you shot anyway (40 minutes).
  4. Cut 3: a story or quick post with one quote or behind-the-scenes photo (10 minutes).

In the calendar, schedule the anchor first and the cuts after it. If the anchor slips, the cuts slip with it, which is exactly what you want to see early. Grouping the cuts into one editing block is task batching applied to content.

Common mistakes

  • Planning only publish dates. The single biggest cause of missed posts.
  • A cadence set on a motivated day. Plan for your busiest month, not your best one.
  • Making each post from scratch. Batch shoots and writing sessions; reuse one long piece across channels.
  • No buffer posts. Keep 2–3 evergreen posts ready for the week everything goes wrong.
  • Ideas living in five apps. Notes, DMs, screenshots, voice memos. One idea list, reviewed weekly.

Questions, answered.

How far ahead should a content calendar go?

Four weeks of publish dates is a good default, with a rough idea list for the quarter. Further than that, plans go stale.

What should a content calendar include?

Publish date, channel, pillar, working title and status. Add production blocks (script, shoot, edit) on your real calendar.

How many posts a week should I plan?

As many as you can sustain for 12 weeks in your busiest month. For solo creators with other work, two or three is common.

Should I use a spreadsheet or an app?

A spreadsheet is fine for publish dates. The production work needs to be on the calendar you actually live in, or it won’t happen.

What are content pillars?

Three or four recurring themes your audience expects, rotated through the calendar so the mix stays balanced.
