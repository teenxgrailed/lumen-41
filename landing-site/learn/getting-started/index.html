Your first day in Needt

In about 20 minutes you can go from sign-up to a planned day in Needt: connect one calendar, type in today’s tasks with estimates, auto-schedule them around your meetings, and install the app. This guide walks through each step in order, with what to type.

1. Sign up (2 min)

  1. Go to use.needt.app and create an account.
  2. Confirm your email. That unlocks 14 days of Pro, no card needed. After that you can stay on Free or pick Pro or Lifetime on pricing.
  3. Pick a theme, dark or light. You can change it later.

Coming from Todoist or Notion? You can bring tasks over under Settings → Data → Import & export (Todoist CSV/backup, Notion Markdown & CSV export). Titles, due dates, projects and notes come with them. See Todoist and Notion.

2. Connect a calendar (3 min)

Needt plans around your meetings, so it needs to see them. Connect Google Calendar, Outlook/Microsoft 365, iCloud or any CalDAV calendar from Settings. Free includes one calendar; Pro is unlimited.

Connect the calendar that holds most of your commitments first. If work and personal are split, connect both during the Pro trial and see whether you need both. Setup notes: Google Calendar, Outlook, iCloud.

3. Add today’s tasks (5 min)

Type tasks the way you’d say them. Needt parses the estimate, deadline and project:

  • send the report, 30 min, by Friday #Clients
  • fix checkout bug, 45 min, today #Website, urgent
  • Spanish lesson, 30 min #Spanish, low energy
  • book plumber, 10 min #Home

The fields that matter most on day one: estimate (minutes) and deadline. Add priority (Low/Medium/High/Urgent) and energy (Low/Medium/High) when you know them. Subtasks and reminders can wait.

No idea how long something takes? Guess 30 or 60 minutes. You’ll calibrate within a week, and the plan rebuilds when you’re wrong.

4. Auto-schedule (1 min)

Let Needt auto-schedule your tasks. It places them into free time around your meetings, respecting deadlines, priority and energy. Check the result on Home (today) or in Calendar (day/week/month). Events and scheduled tasks appear in one view.

When a meeting moves or a task runs over, the rest of the day rebuilds and Needt shows what moved; you confirm. The free plan includes 15 auto-scheduled tasks a month. How it works in depth: auto-scheduling explained.

Worked example: Alex’s first Tuesday

Alex signs up Monday night, connects Google Calendar (stand-up 09:30–09:45, client call 11:00–12:00) and types five tasks. Tuesday’s plan after auto-scheduling:

TimeItemSource
08:30–09:15Fix checkout bug #WebsiteTask (urgent, due today)
09:15–09:25Book plumber #HomeTask
09:30–09:45Stand-upGoogle Calendar
09:45–10:15Send the report #ClientsTask
11:00–12:00Client callGoogle Calendar
13:00–14:30Homepage copy #WebsiteTask (high energy)
16:30–17:00Spanish lessonTask (low energy)

At 10:40 the client moves the call to 14:00. The day rebuilds: homepage copy shifts to 11:00–12:30, and Alex confirms the change in one tap.

5. Try Ask (2 min)

Ask is the AI agent inside Needt. It can read your tasks and calendar and act on them. Good first prompts:

What’s overdue?
Re-plan my afternoon. I need to leave at 16:00.
Turn this email into tasks.

Free has a small monthly allowance; Pro and Lifetime include it. You can also bring your own AI key on any plan. The hosted agent uses providers that don’t retain or train on your data (security).

6. Install the app (2 min)

Needt installs as an app on Mac, Windows, iPhone, iPad and Android straight from the browser. On iPhone or iPad, open use.needt.app in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. In Chrome or Edge on desktop, use the install option in the address bar or browser menu. Step-by-step for each device on the download page. Native iOS and Android apps are in development.

7. Optional: connect your AI assistant (3 min)

If you use Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor or another MCP client, connect it to Needt’s remote MCP server at https://mcp.needt.app/u/your-name. Sign in with OAuth, or create a personal token under Settings → Data → Personal API. Your assistant can then create tasks, list them, run the scheduler and reschedule.

Your first week

Day one gets you a plan. These small habits over the first week make it stick:

  • Days 1–3: add every new task with an estimate, even rough. Notice which estimates were wrong.
  • Day 2: connect your mailbox (Gmail, Outlook or IMAP; up to 3 on Pro) and turn one email into a task.
  • Day 3: create a project per area of life, like Clients, Website, Home, and try the Kanban or Flow view in Workspace.
  • Day 4: start one Doc (meeting notes or a plan) and link it to a project. The meeting notes template is a good first page.
  • Day 5: run a 15-minute review and plan next week.

First-week mistakes to skip

  • Importing everything on day one. Bring in active work only. Old someday lists can wait.
  • Tasks without estimates. They can’t be scheduled well. A rough guess is enough.
  • Everything marked urgent. Use urgent for today-or-it-breaks only.
  • Not connecting the calendar. Without meetings, the plan will double-book you.
  • Skipping the weekly look. Fifteen minutes on Friday keeps the system honest; see weekly review.

Questions, answered.

Is Needt free?

Yes, there is a free plan with one calendar, one board and 15 auto-scheduled tasks a month. Every new account gets 14 days of Pro after confirming email, no card required.

Which calendars can I connect?

Google Calendar, Outlook/Microsoft 365, iCloud and any CalDAV calendar. Free connects one; Pro is unlimited.

Is there a mobile app?

Needt installs as an app on iPhone, iPad and Android from the browser. Native iOS and Android apps are in development.

Can I import from Todoist or Notion?

Yes. Settings → Data → Import & export accepts a Todoist export and a Notion export. Tasks keep titles, due dates, projects and notes.

Can I export my data?

Yes, as JSON, any time. You can also delete your account; there is a grace period.

Do I need an AI key to use Ask?

No. Free includes a small monthly allowance and Pro includes Ask. You can optionally bring your own AI key on any plan.
